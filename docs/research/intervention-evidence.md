# Evidence and pre-declared study protocol

Reviewed 9 November 2026. The user authorized revision of the mathematical paper and investigation of defensible interventions to pursue mean clearance below 180 seconds. The previous engine, manuscript and 100-run output are retained separately. No earlier simulator branch is used.

## Evidence and transfer limits

- Galea, Xie & Lawrence (2014), *Experimental and survey studies on the effectiveness of dynamic signage systems*, Fire Safety Science 11, 1129–1143, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3801/IAFSS.FSS.11-1129). Controlled experiments reported 38% detection for conventional signs and 77% for the tested active design. These support attention-enhancing LED guidance; they do not calibrate strips, range, compliance or crowd occlusion at Bailey. Retain the original model's 0.77 detection and 0.80 conditional acceptance as explicit transfer assumptions. A static route message can still have active/pulsing illumination; static route assignment is distinct from an unilluminated conventional sign.
- Galea et al. (2017), *Evaluating the effectiveness of an improved active dynamic signage system using full scale evacuation trials*, Fire Safety Journal 91, 908–917, [author repository](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/16552/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.firesaf.2017.03.022). Full-scale station trials redirected 66% toward the intended exit with consistent active signs and voice messaging. This motivates coordinated public messages and incomplete compliance, not perfect obedience or a 66% time reduction.
- Wang et al. (2023), *Influence of pre-bottleneck diversion devices on pedestrian flow*, [full author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.01296v1), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/acb42c). In their experiment safety barriers at 1 m and 3 m increased evacuation times by 0.88% and 2.67%; different devices performed differently. This contradicts assigning a universal barrier benefit. Here belts change topology, width and detour only; no percentage flow bonus is introduced.
- Feliciani et al. (2020), *Systematic experimental investigation of the obstacle effect during non-competitive and extremely competitive evacuations*, [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-72733-w). Effects depend on geometry and competitiveness, and local congestion can move to the channels around an obstacle. Its opaque cylinder experiments are evidence of limited transferability, not a proposed installation: only low belts/open rails preserving sightlines are studied here.
- Chen et al. (2023), *Effect of S-shaped guardrails on pedestrian crowd flows in bottleneck areas*, [publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925753522003599), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssci.2022.106020). The study used 97 volunteers and a specific S-shaped arrangement. It does not justify a generic rail speed multiplier or Bailey dimensions; the full article was not accessible during this review.
- Feliciani, Murakami & Nishinari (2018), *A universal function for capacity of bidirectional pedestrian streams*, [PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0208496). Lane organization and counterflow conflicts motivate shared capacity. The exact discrete queue rule remains a computational convention. The supplied building brief explicitly describes two stair flights with barriers dividing two lanes; those lanes should not acquire mutually incompatible directions at a fictitious graph boundary.

## Model corrections, independent of policy selection

The final engine coordinates the two existing rear-stair lanes across their entrance, both flights and lower approach. Gate queues on opposing faces are distinct FIFO queues sharing one total service budget. An upstream blocked request must not stop the opposite stream leaving the passage. These corrections address the identified discretization deadlock; they create neither storage nor capacity, and are applied to all experimental arms. The same lane continuity applies to both stepped cross-aisles. Existing occupants can continue along a draining lane while new admissions are stopped; otherwise logical lane reassignment can freeze its own drainage. Regression cases include former stair stalls 1022, 1023, 1033 and 1041 and cross-aisle stalls 3011, 3015 and 3020. The current-state commitment rule applies the existing 8 s improvement criterion to guidance received after movement begins and commits final exterior crossings. Corrections were made from inspected state/transition defects, not by changing capacities or speeds.

## Intervention design space

- Six managers remain the minimum. Fixed alternative messages/posts and additional managers can be tested; their effects must be distinguished from LED/barrier effects.
- Added LED strips occupy existing walkways. Geometric visibility, fixed notice/acceptance tendencies, 3 s activation, and limited range remain. Dynamic routing uses the original delayed observations, 8 s improvement criterion and 20 s hold.
- Low belts/open rails can partition the outer corridor between exit service areas, as explicitly proposed by the contest brief. They cannot obscure stage or exit sightlines, occupy door thresholds, add exits or increase capacity. Fully spanning an internal corridor link closes that connection; partial installations lose projected width and incur detour. Wheelchair connectivity and all occupied origin reachability are validated. This geometric screening does not constitute approval to install a device in an actual egress route.
- Population (1,329 including three wheelchair users), age/speed/response distributions, service/headway parameters, physical widths and assistance rules are held fixed in the main study. Sensitivities are labelled separately.

## Experiment and selection protocol

Use pilot seeds beginning at 3001 for design exploration. Record every tested candidate and retain failed or slower outcomes. Select a fixed layout based on pilot performance and feasibility. Then freeze the engine and policy and run 100 paired fresh audiences, seeds 5001–5100, for the baseline and intervention comparisons. Do not use validation-seed outcomes to revise the selected layout. If the target is not attained, report that result; further redesign requires a separate independent validation block.

Factorial comparisons keep manager staffing, placement and messages identical while toggling LEDs and barriers. A separate original-post reference identifies changes due to manager coordination. Report the unconditional mean only when all runs clear, sample SD, confidence interval for mean/paired difference, 180 s success with Wilson interval, exit use, queue/density indicators and accessible outcomes. Never substitute the completed-only mean for censored performance. The 900 s horizon and 0.1 s main time step remain fixed; 0.0125 s refinement measures numerical sensitivity and cannot be selected merely because it is faster.

The target is the average of per-replication full-house clearance maxima, not average individual exit time. A mean below 180 s is not a guarantee that each evacuation meets 180 s. Monte Carlo uncertainty does not include uncertainty in reconstructed geometry or behavioral transfer.
